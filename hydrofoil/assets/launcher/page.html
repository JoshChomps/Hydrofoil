    Boat editor

    Change the weight, where it sits, and the foils. The numbers on the right are worked out from the boat's trim at each speed, before anything flies. Save to relaunch the 3D view with the new boat; the controller gains and schedule are re-derived for it.

    Foil shape in this model: rectangular planform and one section (NACA 0009), so span, chord, aspect ratio, flap size and incidence are what changes. Taper, sweep, twist and other sections are not modelled. Moving weight up or down is reported but not flown (the sim has no vertical CG shift).

    Autotuner

    Flies every candidate through the same five tests (takeoff from rest, head sea, following sea, rider moving, and a head sea on the noisy sensors the real boat would have) and scores it: height error, peaks, overshoot, settling time, flap rate and drive limits, with failures heavily penalised. It works in stages: the current gains, then textbook methods, then a search that refines the best of them.

    Methods and sources

    PID: a relay feedback test in the sim on each loop (Åström and Hägglund, Automatica 20(5), 1984) gives the ultimate gain and period, then the Ziegler–Nichols rules (Trans. ASME 64, 1942) and Tyreus–Luyben (Ind. Eng. Chem. Res. 31(11), 1992). Skogestad's SIMC rules (J. Process Control 13, 2003) need a step-response model, which the open-loop unstable heave loop cannot give, so they are not used.

    LQR: Bryson's rule (Bryson and Ho, Applied Optimal Control, 1975), then an iterative re-weighting that tightens whatever overshot its target. That loop is this project's own heuristic, not a published method.

    Refinement: a cross-entropy search (Rubinstein, Methodology and Computing in Applied Probability 1, 1999) started from the best of the above.
